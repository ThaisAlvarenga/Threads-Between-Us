# Why use a digital garden to document a Master's Project?

This research sits at the intersection of methodological pluralism (valuing different epistemic traditions) and cultural empowerment (giving legitimacy to how Honduran institutions already do things. This includes research communication). 

I’m not just studying what research says in Honduras—I’m interested in how research is told and shared, and how those practices shape publics, communities, and agency (maybe also the mental models that people have around AI/ HCI/Computing/Technoculture, etc).

Mental models in Human-Computer Interaction (in HCI) refer to the internal representations that users form about how a system works based on their past experiences and understanding. These models influence user expectations and interactions with digital products, guiding how they navigate and utilize interfaces effectively

A mental model is what the user believes about the system in question—including how it behaves, what they can do with it, and what will change in the system if they take certain actions.

Interaction Design Foundation - IxDF. (2016, June 2). What are Mental Models?. Interaction Design Foundation - IxDF. https://www.interaction-design.org/literature/topics/mental-models


Throughout the  research process for this project, I've gone off on many tangents and rabiit holes. The amount of information that I compiled over the course of two years has been so overwhelming that I had to device ways to organize this information to make it useful for my purposes.

Amongst the many research tools that I've leveraged, Obsidian was one of the most essential for both storing and creating knowledge. It is a system of hyperlinked notes that can be used to create connections between different notes. There are great videos that explain how these types of system work. 

The information architecture of wikis ==reflects how contributors _think_. It is not a fixed ontology.==
Similarly, a digital garden serves as a reflection of how the particular person thinks and forms connections between literature, thoughts and projects.
